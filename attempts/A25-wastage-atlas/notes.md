# A25-wastage-atlas — a CONTINUUM wastage atlas: certified dead area over BOXES of placements

Owner: postdoc (computational geometry / high-throughput exact computation). 2026-08-20.
Status: **done-success** (~5 h). Consumer: **A24** (`query.py` is the import surface).

A23 built a sound dead-region certifier and measured it at *points* (one fixed placement, or a
0.02 × 0.02 × 2° core). A case-tree node does not fix a placement — it fixes a **box**. A25 turns
A23's oracle into a **lookup table over boxes**: for every cell of a (centre × tilt) grid, a rational
lower bound on the area that ANY square placed in that cell strands, valid uniformly over the cell,
plus the explicit list of rectangles realising it (so a caller can check the interior-disjointness
side condition of the budget lemma before adding several of them).

---

## 1. What is certified (semantics of an atlas entry)

Container `[0,s]²`, s rational. An atlas **cell** is a box `K = [t0,t1] × [cx0,cx1] × [cy0,cy1]`
(t = tan θ/2), i.e. the A13/A14 YES-node hypothesis *"some packing square has these parameters"*.
The entry stores a rational `dead_lb(K)` and a list of axis-aligned rational rectangles `R_1..R_m`
with the machine-checked property

> for **every** placement P with parameters in K: the `R_i` are pairwise interior-disjoint,
> interior-disjoint from P, and **dead for {P}** — no unit square inside `[0,s]²` with interior
> disjoint from P meets `int R_i` — and `Σ area(R_i) = dead_lb(K)`.

Combined with A23's Lemma 1 (monotonicity: dead for H ⇒ dead for any H′ ⊇ H) and Lemma 2 (budget:
pairwise interior-disjoint dead regions satisfy `Σ area ≤ s² − n` in any n-packing containing H),
this gives the **kill test** the tree can run:

> **Kill rule.** If a node's hypothesis puts squares in cells `K_1..K_k` and the region lists
> `R(K_1),…,R(K_k)` are pairwise interior-disjoint **across the k squares**, then any 11-packing
> satisfying the node obeys `Σ_i dead_lb(K_i) ≤ s² − 11`; exceeding it kills the node.

The cross-square disjointness is *not* automatic and is the caller's obligation — `query.lookup`
returns the rectangles in the query frame and `query.disjoint_regions` checks it. (Within one entry
disjointness is free: the regions are tiles of a common dyadic grid, see §4.)

Note the k squares must be **distinct** squares of the packing; `analyse.can_coexist` also imposes
the necessary condition that the centre boxes admit distinct interior-disjoint squares at all
(two interior-disjoint unit squares have centre distance ≥ 1 exactly — each has support ≥ ½ towards
a separating line).

## 2. D4 convention (and a correction to the brief)

The container symmetry group acting on placements `(c, θ)` is D4 of order 8, generated by

* `r : (x,y) ↦ (s−y, x)` with **θ unchanged** — a 90° rotation of the container maps a unit square to
  a unit square of the same tilt, because θ is only defined mod 90°;
* `m : (x,y) ↦ (y,x)` with **θ ↦ 90°−θ**.

A fundamental domain is therefore **{centre in the QUADRANT [0,s/2]²} × {θ ∈ [0°,45°]}**:
the four rotations bring any centre into the quadrant, and the residual stabiliser of the quadrant is
the diagonal reflection `m`, which is exactly what `θ ≤ 45°` consumes. **The brief asked for an
octant *and* θ ≤ 45°; that would be a double count** (|D4| = 8 = 4 · 2, not 8 · 2) and would leave
half of the placement space with no certificate. The atlas therefore stores the quadrant; `query.py`
maps a query box through all 8 group elements, keeps the parts landing in the stored domain, and
returns the minimum over every atlas cell they meet (0 if any part is uncovered) — a lower bound
valid uniformly over the whole query box. (Round-trip test in `python query.py <atlas>`: the corner
cell, its 90°-rotation, and its mirror-with-θ→90−θ all return the same 0.2617.)

Cells are indexed by the *grid* box but the stored `cx`,`cy` are the **admissible clip**
`[max(lo, wLo/2), min(hi, s−wLo/2)]` with `wLo = min_{t-box}(cos+sin)`; every admissible placement of
the grid cell lies in the clip, so clipping strengthens nothing and only helps the certifier.

Pair entries carry `A` and `B` sub-boxes instead of `cx/cy/t/theta_deg`, and a `realisable` flag

## 4. The efficiency idea, and what it cost

**We never need the maximal dead area — only a certified lower bound big enough to be useful.**
So per cell:

1. **A small number of candidate windows**, not the whole container: for a single square, the
   wall/corner shadow strips of A23 §4a (a wall counts only if the square could come within
   `gap_max = 1` of it — a gap ≥ 1 admits an upright square, so nothing there can be dead); for a
   pair, additionally the inter-square window. Windows are made pairwise **disjoint** by rectangle
   subtraction *before* tiling, which is what makes the summed regions interior-disjoint for free.
2. **A coarse tiling**: `cell0 = 1/8` refined at most once to `min_cell = 1/16`, versus A23's 1/64
   quadtree.
3. **Level order + a work budget**: every tile is screened at `cell0` (ONE `quick_cover` probe)
   before anything is refined, and refinement is applied only to tiles that touch a certified-dead
   tile — the wedge boundary — plus tiles clipped by the core hull. A global work budget (1200
   certifier calls for singles, 2200 for pairs) stops the cell.

Every one of these caps only *drops* tiles, so the reported number stays a certified lower bound.

**Measured cost.** Coarse single sweep: **2.94 s CPU per cell** (1310 cells, 773 s wall on 5
processes); fine single sweep 4.0 s/cell; pair sweep **8.85 s CPU per pair** (330 pairs, 978 s wall
on 3 processes). A23's estimate for one corner box done exhaustively was ~1 CPU-day; the same object
(`corner_box`, core `[0.72,0.74]² × [39°,41°]`, s = 3.85) now takes **1.3 s** and returns 0.4141
where A23's exhaustive 1/64 map returned 0.5285 — i.e. **~2000× faster for 78 % of the value**.
Resolution ladder on that cell: 1/16 → 0.4141 (1.3 s), 1/32 → 0.4316 (4.9 s), 1/64 → 0.4434 (19 s);
the marginal value of refinement is small, which is why 1/16 is the default.

Two implementation notes that mattered:
* `quick_cover` (the *negative* filter — finding a legal cover proves a tile is not dead) is run
  against the **midpoint realisation** of the core box, not against its axis-aligned hull. Exhibiting
  a square legal for one realisation of the core already refutes deadness for the core-box
  hypothesis, so this is sound, and it cut interior-cell cost by 7× (the hull is 1.55 × 1.55 and made
  the filter useless, forcing 1500-box certifications on obviously-alive tiles). The certified areas
  were bit-identical before and after the change — a useful consistency check, since a *dead* tile
  can never admit a cover legal for any realisation.
* `quick_cover`'s SAT axes are now each obstacle's own edge normals (A23 used the midpoint frame,
  which is wrong for a non-square obstacle; it was sound but blunt).

## 6. The dominant effect: the atlas value is a THRESHOLD in core-box size

Same centre (1.275, 1.275), s = 3.84, same certifier, varying only the hypothesis box:

| core box (centre side × tilt) | θ ≈ 1.5° | θ ≈ 31.5° |
|---|---|---|
| point placement (A23 semantics) | 1.8848 | 1.7422 |
| 0.15 × 0.15 × 3° (this atlas)   | 1.5000 | **0.3203** |
| 0.075 × 0.075 × 3°              | –      | **1.4912** |
| 0.0375 × 0.0375 × 3°            | –      | 1.5361 |
| 0.010 × 0.010 × 3°              | –      | 1.6514 |
| 0.010 × 0.010 × 1°              | –      | 1.6836 |

**Halving the centre box multiplies the mid-tilt value by 4.7.** The tilt width is almost free
(3° → 1° buys +2 %); it is the *centre* uncertainty that matters, and there is a threshold between
0.075 and 0.15 for tilted squares (an upright square degrades gracefully, 1.88 → 1.50, because its
moat is bounded by axis-parallel edges that merely shift). Two mechanisms: (i) tiles must be
interior-disjoint from *every* realisation, so a boundary layer of width ≈ h around the union of
realisations is lost outright (≈ 0.8 of area at h = 0.15); (ii) a tilted square's wedges against the
wall open an escape for a same-tilted covering square in *some* realisation, which kills the tile for
*all* of them.

**Advice for A24: branch the centre to 0.075 before querying the atlas at mid tilt.** A separate fine
atlas (h = 3/40, 5° tilt bins, θ ∈ [15°,45°]) is shipped for exactly this
(`results/atlas_single_fine_3.84.json`).

## 7. Pair atlas (s = 3.84, fine grid h = 3/40, 5° bins, θ ∈ [15°,45°], equal tilt bins)

330 pairs at box-centre distance 1.00–1.25 around three anchors (near-corner (0.75,0.79)², wall
(1.2,1.28)×(0.75,0.79), interior (1.2,1.28)²).

* median pair value **1.328**, 188/330 ≥ 1.0, 61/330 ≥ 2.0, **max 3.0805**;
* **superadditive**, as A23 predicted: e.g. on the coarse grid `A(8,8)+δ(8,0)` gives 1.0961 against
  0.270 + 0.178 = 0.448 for the two singles (gain +0.65); `A(8,5)+δ(0,8)` gives 1.4961 against 0.840
  (gain +0.66). The pair's two moats merge into one shadow that no square can enter from either end;
* A23's own `pair_box` (the two adjacent 33° pinwheel squares of the s = 3.86 interior support, cores
  ±0.01, θ ∈ [32.1°,33.9°]) re-measures at **1.7830** (1/16) / **1.8553** (1/32) here versus A23's
  1.3837 — A25's windows reach the wall that A23's hand-chosen sub-window cut off, so this is a
  strict improvement on the published number, not a discrepancy;
* **realisability.** A necessary condition (max centre distance ≥ 1) is not sufficient for two
  *tilted* squares. A grid search over both cores (`feasible.py`) finds an explicitly disjoint
  instance for 276/330 pairs; the other **54 are flagged `realisable: false`** — their dead-area
  statement is true but vacuous, and they are excluded from the min-k analysis below. This is a trap
  worth stating: a pair atlas without a realisability flag will silently manufacture "kills" of empty
  hypotheses.

## 8. Both container sizes, and why the tables coincide

`results/atlas_single_3.84.json` (s = 96/25, budget s²−11 = **3.7456**) and
`results/atlas_single_3.877084.json` (s = 3877084/1000000, budget **4.031780343056**).
The two headline tables are *identical to the printed precision*, and this is correct, not a bug: for
a cell in the lower-left quadrant the entire local geometry (bottom and left wall, the square, the
covering square) is independent of s as long as the far walls are out of reach, which they are for
every cell of the grid. **s enters only through (i) the admissible domain, (ii) which far walls come
into reach, and (iii) the budget.** For the optimality programme this means the atlas is essentially
s-independent in `[3.84, 3.88]` and only the budget moves — the kill test gets *harder* at s′ by
0.286 of budget, which is exactly the 7.6 % margin the k = 3 statement below has to absorb.

## 9. The derived statement — minimum k

`analyse.py` searches (beam search over the D4 orbit of every atlas entry, requiring pairwise
region-disjointness and pairwise placeability) for the largest attainable `Σ dead_lb` using at most k
squares:

| k | best Σ dead_lb, any tilt | best Σ, all squares mid-tilt (θ ≥ 15°) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.4648 | 1.4023 |
| 2 | 3.0805 | 3.0805 |
| 3 | **4.5453** | **4.4828** |
| 4 | 6.0102 | 5.8852 |

> **Theorem (machine-certified, float-interval; the k = 3 witness re-certified exactly).**
> Let s = 96/25. Any case-tree node whose hypothesis places three distinct squares in the cells
> `[1.20,1.35]² × [15°,18°]` and its two 90°-rotations (centres `[2.49,2.64]×[1.20,1.35]` and
> `[2.49,2.64]²`, same tilt range) has certified dead area 3 × 1.4023 = **4.2070 > 3.7456 = s²−11**
> and is therefore **dead**: no packing of 11 unit squares in `[0,3.84]²` has three squares in those
> boxes. The same three cells give 4.2070 > 4.0318 at s′ = 3.877084, so **the kill survives at s′.**
> The strongest k = 3 witness mixes a pair entry with a single: `3.0805 + 1.4023 = 4.4828`.

**k = 3 is the minimum this atlas achieves, and k = 2 is out of reach**: the best two-square value is
3.0805, short of the budget by 0.665 at 3.84 and by 0.951 at s′. Closing that gap needs a factor
1.22 (resp. 1.31) more from a pair — the resolution ladder (§4) offers ~7 %, and shrinking the pair
cores from 0.075 towards A23's 0.02 offers more (A23's *point* pinwheel-pair number was 1.88 with a
much weaker window; our 0.075-core pairs already reach 3.08), so **k = 2 is not obviously impossible,
just not reached here.** That is the single most valuable follow-up.

**Typical case.** The median *positive* mid-tilt coarse-atlas cell is 0.2031, so a node that fixes
mid-tilt squares in generic (0.15-box) cells needs k ≈ 19 — i.e. the test is useless at the coarse
grid in the typical case and decisive only for the near-corner band. On the fine (0.075) grid the
typical mid-tilt value rises into the 0.5–1.5 range and the typical k drops accordingly.

Comparison with A23: A23 killed with k = 4 (the realised 33° pinwheel 4-cycle, dead 6.55) but only
for four **exactly fixed** placements. A25 kills with **k = 3 over continuum boxes** — which is what
a case tree can actually assert.

## 10. Coverage and honest limits

Coarse single atlas at s = 3.84 (1310 admissible cells, 810 of them mid-tilt):

| threshold | all cells | mid-tilt cells (of 810) |
|---|---|---|
| > 0 | 1061 (81 %) | 657 (81 %) |
| ≥ 0.1 | 887 (68 %) | 552 (68 %) |
| **≥ 0.3** | **446 (34 %)** | **286 (35 %)** |
| ≥ 0.5 | 339 (26 %) | 229 (28 %) |
| ≥ 1.0 | 80 (6 %) | 46 (6 %) |

So **≈ 35 % of the mid-tilt placement space carries a useful value (≥ 0.3)** at the coarse grid.
Where the atlas gives ~0 and why:

* **deep interior** (`min(cx0,cy0) ≥ 1.35`, i.e. every wall gap ≥ 1): genuinely 0 — a lone square in
  open space strands nothing, a covering square can always come alongside it. Verified rather than
  assumed on sample cells (full pipeline, 456 tiles, area exactly 0). These cells are screened out by
  the `gap_max = 1` window rule at ~0 cost; the screen is sound because reporting 0 is always sound.
* **flush against a wall** (`d ≈ 0.45–0.6`): small (0.5–0.7) and for θ ≥ 15° often inadmissible.
  Consistent with A23's finding that flush corner squares strand exactly 0.
* **mid-tilt at `d ≈ 1.2` in the coarse grid**: an artefact of the 0.15 box (§6), recovered by the
  fine atlas.
* the whole atlas is **one-sided**: it can only kill, never certify a node alive, and it says nothing
  about Trump-like structures (which must stay under budget, since the packing exists).

**Trust status.** Everything in the sweeps is the A23 certifier in `float` mode (IEEE with outward
safety guards, every rounding on the conservative side) — "certified modulo rounding". On top of
that:
* **exact re-certification**: the top 30 single entries were re-run in pure `Fraction` arithmetic on
  the identical tile lists — **30/30 entries, 0 failed tiles, exact area = float area to the last
  bit** (313 s on 3 processes, `results/exact_s384.json`), including the whole k = 3 witness family.
  Pair top entries likewise (`results/exact_pair384.json`).
* **independent adversarial check** (`adversarial.py`, `adv_pair.py`): random realisations of the
  core box(es) → random *legal* placements by plain float SAT → does any meet a certified tile? Top
  12 single entries: 720 realisations, ~14 000 legal placements, **0 hits**. Top 8 realisable pairs
  (seeded at an explicit disjoint realisation): ~4 900 legal placements, **0 hits**.
* remaining gaps: `min_cell = 1/16` leaves a boundary layer uncounted (a *loss*, not a risk); the
  work budget truncated 0 coarse single cells and a handful of pair cells (flagged
  `stats.truncated`); the D4 reduction and the query's cell-covering logic are argued in §2, not
  machine-checked.
